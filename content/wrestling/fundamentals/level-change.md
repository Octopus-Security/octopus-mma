The level change is the first movement of every shot. Before you can attack the legs, your shoulders have to come down to the height of the opponent's hips, and the level change is how you get there without giving anything away. Bend the knees and the hips drop straight down, with the back angle and the head held where they were. Bend at the waist instead and your head falls below your hips, which is exactly where the opponent wants it for a snap-down or a front headlock. The animation shows the stance, then the level change.

## Steps

1. Start in a good stance, hips at the height you wrestle from.
2. Bend the knees so the hips drop straight down.
3. Keep the back at the same angle it had in the stance; do not fold forward at the waist.
4. Keep the head up and the eyes on the opponent's hips.
5. Keep the elbows in and the hands in front.

## Key Points

- Knees, not waist. The back angle in the level change is the same as in the stance.
- The head stays up. The hips go down; the head does not go down first.
- It should be quick and give nothing away: no wind-up, no shifting of the feet first.
- Change levels at the right distance. Dropping too far away is a level change the opponent can watch.
- Level-change with feints too, so the drop is not always a shot.

## Common Mistakes

- Bending at the waist so the head drops and the hips stay high.
- Looking down at the mat.
- Stepping or rocking backward before dropping, which signals the shot.
- Dropping so low the knees are past 90 degrees and you cannot drive forward.
- Letting the hands drop to the floor.

## Drills

- Level-change drill: from stance, change levels and come back up, 10 at a time, checking the back angle each rep.
- Mirror check: level-change side-on to a mirror and compare the back angle before and after.
- Partner feints: one partner keeps a hand on your head; level-change without the hand being pushed forward.
- Level change into a penetration step, then back to stance.

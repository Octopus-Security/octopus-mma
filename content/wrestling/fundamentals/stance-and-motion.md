Everything in wrestling starts from the stance, and most takedowns are won or lost on stance alone. A good stance keeps your legs away from the opponent's hands, keeps your hips under your shoulders so you cannot be snapped down, and leaves you ready to shoot at any moment. Motion is the stance on the move: you step and follow, step and follow, so you are never caught with your feet crossed or your weight on one leg. ## Steps

1. Feet a little wider than your shoulders, one foot slightly ahead of the other (a staggered stance).
2. Bend the knees until your hips sit low, roughly at the height you would wrestle from.
3. Keep the hips under you and the back straight, leaning forward from the hips, not rounding over at the waist.
4. Head up, eyes on the opponent's hips or chest, elbows in, hands out in front at about knee height.
5. To move forward, step the lead foot first, short and low, then bring the rear foot the same distance.
6. To move back, step the rear foot first, then the lead foot follows.

## Key Points

- The feet never cross and never come together. Each step is followed by the other foot, so the stance is the same after every step.
- Steps are short. Long steps leave you on one leg.
- Hips stay at one height while you move: no bobbing up and down.
- Weight on the balls of the feet, never on the heels.
- Elbows in and hands active; they protect your legs and hand-fight theirs.

## Common Mistakes

- Standing too tall, which leaves the legs open to a shot.
- Bending at the waist instead of the knees: the head drops and you get snapped down.
- Crossing the feet when moving sideways.
- Reaching with the hands so the elbows come away from the body.
- Square feet with no stagger, which leaves nothing to sprawl or shoot from.

## Drills

- Stance holds: 30 seconds in stance, check the back is straight and the head up, rest, repeat.
- Mirror drill: a partner moves forward, back and sideways; you keep the distance and the stance.
- Whistle motion: move around the room in stance; on the whistle, touch the mat with both hands and come straight back up into stance.
- Stance-in-a-line: shuffle forward, back and laterally down a line without the feet crossing or coming together.

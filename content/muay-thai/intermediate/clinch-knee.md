The clinch is where Muay Thai is most different from kickboxing, and the plum (double collar tie) is its strongest position. Both hands go to the back of the opponent's head, one on top of the other, and the forearms press down on their collarbones with the elbows squeezed together. From there you control their posture: pull the head down and they cannot stand up, turn them and they lose their balance, and every pull brings them onto a knee. The animation shows the plum, the pull down with the rear foot stepping back to load, the rear knee driving up into the body, and the foot coming back down with the plum kept.

## Steps

1. From close range, get both hands to the back of the opponent's head, one hand over the other (do not interlace the fingers).
2. Press the forearms into their collarbones and squeeze the elbows together, so their head is trapped between your arms.
3. Pull their head down and step the rear foot back to make space and load the knee.
4. Drive the hips forward and bring the rear knee up into their body, pulling the head down to meet it.
5. Put the foot back down under you and keep the plum. Repeat, or switch legs.

## Key Points

- Hands on the back of the head, not the neck. Hands on top of each other, never interlaced (interlaced fingers are easy to break and to injure).
- The elbows squeeze in. Wide elbows let the opponent swim an arm inside and break the plum.
- Posture: stand tall, hips forward, chest up. You break their posture, not yours.
- The power is the hips driving forward, not the leg alone.
- Keep them off balance between knees by turning them and stepping, so they cannot set their feet.

## Common Mistakes

- Leaning forward so your own head is down and your hips are back.
- Elbows flared out, which gives the opponent the inside position.
- Throwing knees without pulling the head down, so they land on the arms.
- Standing square with no step back, so the knee has no room to travel.
- Holding the clinch still. A clinch where neither person moves is a clinch the referee breaks.

## Drills

- Plum and posture: one partner holds the plum for 30 seconds while the other tries to stand up; switch.
- Pummelling into the plum: swim the arms inside, then lock the plum.
- Knee pads: partner holds a kick shield at the belly, you plum a head-height focus mitt and knee the shield.
- Clinch sparring at light pace: knees to the body only, with points for breaking the opponent's posture.

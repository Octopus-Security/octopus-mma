The long guard is a frame, not a shell. The lead arm reaches out with the glove on the opponent's forehead or face, and the rear glove stays high by your temple. An opponent who walks in to punch meets the long arm first, so their head is pushed back and their punches fall short, while the rear hand covers anything that comes around. It is a defensive tool and also a range-finder: you can feel where the opponent is, turn them, and land a rear hand, an elbow or a kick off it. The animation shows the guard at punching range, the long guard reaching out, the frame stopping the opponent's punch, keeping the frame while they reset, and the return to the guard.

## Steps

1. From the guard at punching range, reach the lead arm out with the glove on the opponent's forehead or face.
2. Keep the rear glove high at your temple, chin down.
3. When they step in to punch, keep the lead arm long and push their head back so the punch falls short.
4. Keep the frame while they reset; use it to turn them or set up a counter.
5. Bring the lead arm back to the guard.

## Key Points

- The frame is on the head, which controls the whole body. A frame on the chest does not stop the punch.
- The lead arm is long but not locked straight, so it can absorb a push.
- Rear glove high and chin down, because hooks come around the long arm.
- The long guard is temporary: use it, then counter or return to the guard.
- Keep the feet in your stance; the frame works with your weight behind it.

## Common Mistakes

- Reaching with the arm while leaning the body forward, which brings you into range.
- Leaving the rear glove low.
- Holding the long guard so long that the opponent times a kick to the open side.
- Pushing with the glove open and fingers out (a thumb in the eye, and illegal in many rule sets).
- Using it at kicking range, where it does nothing against kicks.

## Drills

- Partner: one partner walks forward and throws slow jab-cross; the other uses the long guard to keep them at arm's length.
- Long guard into counter: frame, then land a rear hand or a rear body kick.
- Shadow boxing: alternate between the guard and the long guard, with the rear hand never moving.
- Light sparring rounds where one partner may only defend with the long guard and the teep.

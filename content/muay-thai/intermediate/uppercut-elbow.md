The uppercut elbow comes straight up the middle, between the opponent's gloves, into the chin. It is the elbow for when the opponent is covering with a high, tight guard, or when they duck their head down in the clinch. Like the punching uppercut, it starts with a short dip of the knees and is driven up by the legs. The animation shows the guard, the dip with the elbow dropping low and in, the drive up the centre line, the return, and the guard.

## Steps

1. From the guard at close range, dip by bending the knees; the elbow drops low and tucks in.
2. Drive up with the legs and bring the elbow up the centre line, fist passing your ear.
3. Strike the chin with the point of the elbow.
4. Bring the arm back down to cover.
5. Return to the guard.

## Key Points

- The legs drive it up, not the shoulder.
- Up the centre line, between the opponent's gloves.
- The dip is short. A deep dip is slow and easy to read.
- Keep the other glove on your face.
- In the clinch, it is the answer to an opponent who puts their head down.

## Common Mistakes

- Swinging the elbow in an arc, which turns it into a horizontal elbow that hits the gloves.
- No dip, so all the power is from the arm.
- Throwing it from outside close range.
- Leaning back as the elbow comes up.
- Dropping the other hand.

## Drills

- Shadow boxing: dip and drive, both sides, slowly at first.
- Pads: the holder holds the pad flat, facing down, at chin height.
- Uppercut punch, then uppercut elbow with the same arm on pads.
- Clinch: when the partner drops their head, break one hand free and drive the elbow up into the pad.

The switch kick turns the lead leg into a power kick. A lead-leg kick thrown from the stance has almost no room to turn the hips, so it is quick but light. A quick hop swaps the feet first: the lead foot goes back, the rear foot comes forward, and the leg that was in front now has the full distance of a rear-leg kick. For an orthodox fighter it delivers a left kick to the opponent's liver with rear-leg power. The animation shows the guard, the switch, the kick to the body, the return, and the switch back to the orthodox guard.

## Steps

1. From the guard, make a small hop: the lead foot goes back, the rear foot comes forward. The hands do not move.
2. As you land, immediately pivot on the new lead foot and turn the hips over.
3. Swing the leg through into the body, landing with the shin.
4. Bring the leg back the way it came.
5. Land in the switched stance and switch back to your normal guard.

## Key Points

- The switch is small and quick. A big jump is slow and announces the kick.
- Do not pause between the switch and the kick: they are one movement.
- Keep the hands still and high during the switch; it is the moment you are most open.
- Turn the hips over as in the body kick. The switch only gives the hips room to work.
- Use it as a fake too: switch and teep, or switch and do nothing, so the switch does not always mean a kick.

## Common Mistakes

- Jumping high instead of a low, short switch.
- Pausing after the switch, which gives the opponent time to check.
- Dropping the hands during the hop.
- Kicking with the leg only, no hip turn.
- Switching from too far away so the kick lands with the foot.

## Drills

- Switch footwork alone: switch and switch back, 20 reps, hands still.
- Switch kick on the heavy bag, slow then at speed.
- Pads: jab, cross, switch kick.
- Fakes: switch-teep, switch-kick, switch-nothing, called by the pad holder.

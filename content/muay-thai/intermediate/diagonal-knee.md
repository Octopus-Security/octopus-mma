The diagonal knee is the rear knee thrown at an angle. Where the straight knee drives up the middle, the diagonal knee comes from the side, around the opponent's elbows and into the ribs or the liver. The rear leg loads back and out, the hips turn over the way they would for a body kick, and the knee arcs in at about 45 degrees while the upper body leans away for balance. It works at kicking range and in the clinch, especially when the opponent's elbows are covering the middle. ## Steps

1. From the guard, cock the rear leg back and out to the side; the hips begin to turn away.
2. Turn the hips over, pivoting on the ball of the lead foot, and swing the knee in at about 45 degrees to the ribs.
3. Lean the upper body away from the knee to balance, keeping the lead hand high.
4. Strike with the point of the knee, toes pointed down.
5. Put the foot back down behind you and return to the guard.

## Key Points

- The angle is the point: the knee goes around the elbows, not into them.
- The hips turn over, as in a body kick. Without the hip turn it is only a leg swing.
- Toes pointed, which tightens the knee joint into a hard point.
- Lean away from the knee to balance it, but keep looking at the opponent.
- In the clinch, pull the opponent's head to the opposite side, which opens the ribs.

## Common Mistakes

- Swinging the knee flat across with no upward drive.
- Not turning the hips, so the knee lands with the thigh instead of the point.
- Dropping the hands as you lean back.
- Over-rotating so you end up with your back to the opponent.
- Throwing it straight at the elbows, where a straight knee would have done the same job.

## Drills

- Slow-motion knees on the heavy bag, at 45 degrees, checking the hip turn each rep.
- Kick shield: the holder angles the shield to the side; you land the knee on its flat face.
- Clinch: in the plum, pull the head to one side and throw the diagonal knee to the open side.
- Straight knee, then diagonal knee with the same leg, on the bag.

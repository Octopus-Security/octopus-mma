The horizontal elbow is the most common elbow in Muay Thai. It is thrown at very close range, where a punch has no room, and it cuts rather than pushes: the sharp point of the elbow slices across the eyebrow, the temple or the jaw. Mechanically it is close to a short hook with the arm folded, so the same hip and shoulder turn drive it. The animation shows the guard, the step in with the elbow rising to shoulder height, the slice across, the follow-through and cover, and the guard.

## Steps

1. From the guard, step in to close range.
2. Raise the elbow to shoulder height with the fist by your ear, forearm roughly parallel to the floor.
3. Turn the hips and shoulders together and slice the point of the elbow across at eye height.
4. Follow through, then bring the arm back to cover your face.
5. Return to the guard.

## Key Points

- Strike with the point of the elbow, or just in front of it on the forearm. That is what cuts.
- The turn of the hips and shoulders is the power. The arm stays folded and rides the turn.
- Keep the other glove on your face; at elbow range the opponent can elbow back.
- Close the distance first. An elbow thrown from punching range is a miss.
- It comes after a punch or out of the clinch, when the opponent is already close.

## Common Mistakes

- Swinging the arm on its own with no hip turn.
- Dropping the elbow low, so it hits the shoulder or the glove.
- Throwing from too far out and reaching.
- Leaving the arm out after the strike instead of covering.
- Turning the head away and losing sight of the opponent.

## Drills

- Shadow boxing: jab, then step in with a horizontal elbow, both sides.
- Pads: the holder holds a pad flat at eye height, facing the side; land the point of the elbow.
- Hook into elbow: a hook on the pad, then step in and elbow the same pad.
- Clinch entries: break the plum, then step back slightly and slice the elbow.

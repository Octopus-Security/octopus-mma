The Muay Thai stance looks taller and squarer than a boxing stance, and for a reason: an opponent can kick, knee and clinch as well as punch. The weight sits mostly on the rear leg so the lead leg is free to lift for a check or a teep without a weight shift first. The hips face the opponent more than in boxing, which makes the rear kick and rear knee faster and keeps both shins available to block. The gloves sit high, around the eyebrows, because elbows and head kicks come in above where a boxing guard protects.

## Steps

1. Stand with your feet about shoulder width apart, then step the left foot forward (orthodox) so the lead toes and rear heel are roughly on one line, a little wider than that for balance.
2. Turn the hips and shoulders only slightly sideways — far squarer than boxing. Both hips should be visible to the opponent.
3. Put about 60–70 percent of your weight on the rear leg. Lift the lead heel slightly so the lead foot is light on the ball.
4. Keep both knees soft, not locked and not sitting low.
5. Raise both gloves to brow height, a fist's width in front of the face, with the elbows down and in, covering the ribs.
6. Tuck the chin and look at the opponent's chest, so you see hands and feet together.

## Key Points

- Weight back is the whole point: the lead leg must be able to lift instantly to check a low kick or throw a teep.
- Stand tall. A low, crouched boxing stance makes your legs a target and slows your kicks.
- Gloves higher than in boxing, at the brow — elbows and head kicks are coming.
- Elbows stay close enough to the body that a body kick lands on the arm, not the ribs.
- Many Thai fighters keep a gentle rhythm, rocking forward and back on the rear leg; stay relaxed rather than rigid.

## Common Mistakes

- Using a bladed boxing stance: the lead leg takes every low kick and the rear kick has far to travel.
- Weight on the lead leg, which makes it impossible to check a kick in time.
- Dropping the hands to chin or chest height, which opens the head to kicks and elbows.
- Locking the knees, or crouching so low that the legs cannot react.
- Feet in a line (as if on a tightrope), which leaves no side-to-side balance.

## Drills

- Mirror work: hold the stance for 30-second rounds, checking hand height, weight distribution and lead heel.
- Rhythm drill: rock gently forward and back on the rear leg for a full round without losing the guard.
- Partner taps: a partner lightly taps your lead thigh with a hand; you lift the check without shifting your weight first.

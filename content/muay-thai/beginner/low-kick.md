The low kick is the rear-leg Thai roundhouse aimed at the outside (or inside) of the opponent's thigh. It is the same mechanics as the body and head kick at a lower height, so it is the first kick a beginner learns. A Thai roundhouse differs from a Taekwondo one: it strikes with the shin, not the foot, and the leg swings through nearly straight like a baseball bat, driven by the hip turning over, instead of snapping out from a knee chamber. The turn of the hips happens toward the camera, so side view shows it as the lean and the leg swinging up.

## Steps

1. Step the lead foot slightly out to the left at an angle and rise onto its ball, turning the heel toward the target.
2. Swing the rear leg up and around, the knee only slightly bent, turning the hips over.
3. Strike the outside of the opponent's thigh with the lower shin, just above the knee to mid-thigh.
4. Let the rear arm swing down and back for counterbalance and power, while the lead glove stays high to cover the face.
5. Return the leg along the same path to the stance (or let it swing through and step down if the kick missed).

## Key Points

- The shin is the weapon, not the foot. Aim to land the bone a hand's width below the knee.
- The pivot on the standing foot is what allows the hips to turn over.
- The kick is a swing, not a snap: keep the leg almost straight and kick through the target.
- Set it up with punches: jab–cross–low kick, or cross–lead hook–low kick.
- The lead glove covers the face: the counter to a low kick is a punch over the top.

## Common Mistakes

- Kicking with the foot or the instep, which hurts you more than the opponent.
- Not pivoting, so the kick is all leg and no hip.
- Throwing it from too far away and landing with the foot.
- Dropping both hands as you kick.
- Kicking straight off the stance without a set-up, so the opponent checks it.

## Drills

- Slow kicks: a full round of slow-motion kicks on the bag, checking the pivot and the rear arm swing.
- Pads: 1–2–low kick, then lead hook–low kick.
- Partner: one partner holds a thigh pad on the lead leg; the other lands controlled low kicks.
- Bag: ten kicks each side, returning to stance every time before the next one.

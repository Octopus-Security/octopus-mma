The uppercut ("mat seuy") rises vertically between the opponent's gloves to the chin, or into the solar plexus. It is especially useful in Muay Thai because the high, tight guard covers the side of the head well but leaves a gap straight up the middle, and because an opponent who leans forward to clinch walks onto it. The animation shows the rear uppercut.

## Steps

1. From the stance, bend both knees slightly and drop the rear shoulder a little, keeping the glove at the chin.
2. Drive up with the legs and turn the rear hip forward.
3. Let the rear fist rise on the centre line, palm facing you, elbow bent.
4. Land under the chin or into the body, then return the glove straight to the brow.
5. The lead glove stays high throughout.

## Key Points

- The power comes from the legs straightening, not from the arm.
- Keep the dip small. A big dip is a telegraph and drops your head into knee range.
- Keep the elbow bent; a straight-arm uppercut is a weak scooping push.
- It is a close-range punch: use it when the opponent is leaning in or reaching to clinch.

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the hand to the waist before throwing it.
- Leaning back and scooping instead of driving up.
- Throwing it from too far away.
- Leaving the lead glove low.

## Drills

- Pads: rear uppercut–lead hook at close range.
- Bag: uppercut into rear straight knee, using the uppercut to lift the head.
- Partner: one partner leans forward reaching for a clinch; the other lands a light uppercut to the pad held at the chest.

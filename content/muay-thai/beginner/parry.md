The parry is the most economical defence against a straight punch: instead of absorbing the jab on the guard, the rear glove meets it just in front of the face and knocks it down and across, so it misses and the opponent's arm is out of the way. Because you never take the hit, you can counter at once, usually with your own jab or a teep. The animation shows the opponent's jab, the rear-hand parry and a counter jab.

## Steps

1. From the stance, watch the opponent's chest and shoulders, not the hands.
2. As their jab comes, move the rear glove a few centimetres forward to meet it in front of your face.
3. Slap it down and slightly across with a short wrist-and-forearm movement.
4. Return the glove to the brow immediately.
5. Counter into the opening, with a jab, cross or teep, before their arm returns.

## Key Points

- Small movement. The glove travels a few centimetres, not out to meet the punch.
- Parry late, at the face, so you cannot be faked out of position.
- The parrying glove comes straight back to the brow.
- Parry and counter together: the parry creates the opening only for a moment.

## Common Mistakes

- Reaching out toward the punch, which leaves the face open to a cross.
- Swiping with the whole arm, so the glove is far from the face afterward.
- Parrying every feint.
- Parrying without countering, which gives up the advantage.

## Drills

- Partner: one partner jabs slowly; the other parries. Switch every round, then add speed.
- Parry-jab: parry, then counter with a jab to the pad.
- Parry-teep: parry the jab, then teep to the belly.
- Pad holder taps a pad toward your face at random; parry each one.

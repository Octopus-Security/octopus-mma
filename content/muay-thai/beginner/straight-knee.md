The straight knee ("khao trong") is the simplest knee strike and one of the most powerful techniques in Muay Thai. The rear knee drives straight forward and up into the body (or the face, if the head is pulled down), and the hips push forward behind it so the whole body weight is in the strike. It is thrown at range against an opponent who walks forward, and from the clinch. The animation shows it with the hands reaching forward to pull the opponent's head down onto it.

## Steps

1. From the stance, reach forward with both hands toward the opponent's head or shoulders.
2. Rise onto the ball of the lead foot.
3. Drive the rear knee straight up the centre line, toes pointed down, heel tucked to the buttock.
4. Push the hips forward and lean the shoulders back, pulling down with the hands at the same moment.
5. Lower the foot back behind you to the stance.

## Key Points

- The hips drive the knee: push the belt buckle forward into the target.
- Point the toes down; it tightens the leg and keeps the point of the knee leading.
- Pull with the hands as the knee rises, so the two forces meet.
- Stand tall on the ball of the standing foot.
- Against an opponent walking in, the knee meets them; you do not need to chase.

## Common Mistakes

- Lifting the knee without pushing the hips, so it is a weak lift instead of a strike.
- Bending forward at the waist, which pulls the hips back.
- Toes flexed up, landing with the thigh.
- Hands left at your sides with nothing to pull or guard with.

## Drills

- Bag: hold the bag at head height and drive straight knees alternately for a round.
- Pads: the holder holds the pad at the belly; jab–cross–rear knee.
- Partner: the partner walks forward; you frame with both hands and land a light knee to the belly pad.

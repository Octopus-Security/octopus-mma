The teep is a straight push kick, often called the jab of the legs. It keeps the opponent at range, stops them walking forward, breaks their rhythm and wins points in Thai scoring. The lead teep is the faster, more common one; the rear teep travels further and pushes harder. Unlike a front snap kick, the teep does not whip from the knee: the knee comes up, then the hips push the foot straight through the target and the leg retracts along the same line. ## Steps

1. From the stance, lift the knee of the kicking leg straight up toward your chest, toes pulled back.
2. Push the hips forward and extend the leg straight out, striking with the ball of the foot (or the whole sole for a push).
3. Lean the shoulders back just enough to counterbalance; stay on the ball of the standing foot.
4. Pull the knee straight back to the chest along the same line.
5. Put the foot back down in your stance, or step it forward if you want to follow up.

## Key Points

- Knee first, then the foot. Lifting the knee is what keeps the teep straight and hard to catch.
- The power is the hip thrust, not the leg alone.
- Aim at the belly or the hip of the lead leg as the opponent steps forward: it stops them.
- Retract fast; a teep left out is easy to catch.
- Keep the hands up; the lead-side hand may drop slightly for balance, the other stays on the brow.

## Common Mistakes

- Swinging the leg up like a football kick with no knee chamber.
- Leaning too far back, which takes away the power and leaves you off balance.
- Kicking with the toes instead of the ball of the foot.
- Dropping both hands as the leg goes out.
- Teeping when the opponent is already on top of you; it needs a little distance.

## Drills

- Wall teep: lift the knee, then place the ball of the foot on a wall and push the hips in. Hold, then retract.
- Pads: jab–lead teep, then double lead teep.
- Partner: one partner walks forward steadily; the other stops them with a lead teep to the belly pad every step.
- Pads: catch-and-teep: the pad holder throws a slow body kick, you check it, then teep.

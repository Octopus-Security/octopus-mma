The shin block, or check, is the basic defence against round kicks. Instead of taking a kick on the thigh or the ribs, you lift the leg on the side the kick is coming from and turn the shin out to meet it, so the kicker's shin lands on your shin. The same-side elbow comes down to meet the knee so there is no gap for the kick to come through. It is why the Muay Thai stance keeps the weight on the rear leg: the lead leg must be free to lift instantly. The animation shows a lead-leg check; the outward turn of the knee is toward the camera and is not drawn.

## Steps

1. From the stance, with the weight already on the rear leg, lift the lead knee up and slightly out toward the incoming kick.
2. Turn the shin out so it faces the kick, toes pointed slightly out and the foot tense.
3. Bring the lead elbow down to the knee, so arm and leg make one wall from head to shin.
4. Keep the rear glove high on the brow and the standing knee slightly bent.
5. Absorb the kick, then put the foot straight back down in the stance, ready to counter.

## Key Points

- Lift the knee high: check with the upper shin, near the knee, which is the strongest part.
- The weight must already be on the rear leg; a check that needs a weight shift is late.
- Close the gap between elbow and knee for a body kick.
- Check the kick, do not reach for it with the leg.
- Counter straight away: the kicker is on one leg and turned away from you.

## Common Mistakes

- Lifting the foot instead of the knee, so the kick lands on the soft lower shin or the ankle.
- Leaning back away from the kick, which knocks you over.
- Dropping the hand on the checking side, which opens the head to a high kick.
- Checking every feint, so the opponent learns to punch you when the leg is up.

## Drills

- Partner (slow): the partner throws slow low kicks; you check each one and return to stance.
- Pads: the holder kicks a pad into your raised shin; check, then counter with a cross.
- Check and counter: check, then teep or rear low kick immediately.
- Shadow: check left, check right, cross, for a full round.

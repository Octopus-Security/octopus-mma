The jab is a straight punch with the lead hand. In Muay Thai it does the same job as in boxing — measuring distance, disrupting and setting up combinations — but its most important use is as a set-up for kicks: a jab to the face lifts the opponent's guard and covers the start of a rear kick. "Mat trong" means "straight punch" and covers both the jab and the cross.

## Steps

1. Start in your Muay Thai stance, gloves at the brow.
2. Extend the lead hand straight to the target, turning the fist palm-down just before contact.
3. Let the lead shoulder rise to cover the chin, and take a small step with the lead foot if you need the range.
4. Keep the rear glove on the brow throughout.
5. Bring the hand straight back along the same line to the guard, as fast as it went out.

## Key Points

- Straight out, straight back: the return is part of the punch.
- The rear glove never leaves your face; the counter to a jab is the opponent's cross or kick.
- Weight stays mostly back. A Thai jab does not lunge, so you are still ready to check a kick.
- Use it to blind and to measure: a jab followed by a rear kick is the classic Muay Thai combination.
- It can go to the body too, to bring the opponent's hands down.

## Common Mistakes

- Dropping the jab hand on the way back, which invites a head kick or a right hand over the top.
- Leaning forward onto the lead leg, so you cannot check a low kick.
- Pushing the punch instead of snapping it.
- Throwing it alone with nothing after it.

## Drills

- Shadow boxing: double jab, then jab and step back, focusing on the return to the brow.
- Pads: jab–rear body kick (the "1–kick"), the bread and butter of Thai pad work.
- Partner: one partner jabs lightly, the other parries; switch roles every round.

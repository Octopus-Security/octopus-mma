The body kick is the rear-leg Thai roundhouse aimed at the ribs, the liver (with the left leg) or the arm guarding the body. It is the kick that wins Thai fights on points, because it lands with enormous force on a guard that is not built to stop it. Mechanically it is the low kick thrown higher: the standing foot pivots further and the hips turn over more, so the leg rises to rib height without bending at the knee.

## Steps

1. From the stance, step out slightly with the lead foot at an angle and rise onto its ball.
2. Turn the hips over as the rear leg swings up, the knee nearly straight.
3. Lean the shoulders back and away to counterbalance the leg, without leaning so far you lose sight of the target.
4. Swing the rear arm down and back, keeping the lead glove at the face.
5. Land with the shin across the ribs and kick through, then bring the leg back to the stance.

## Key Points

- Height comes from the hip turn and the pivot, not from lifting the knee.
- Kick through the target, not at it.
- The lean is a counterbalance: the head moves back and away as the leg comes up, and is protected by the lead glove.
- Set it up with the jab or the teep; the opponent's hands come up for a punch and the ribs open.
- A kick that lands on the arm still scores and still hurts; keep throwing it.

## Common Mistakes

- Bending the knee and snapping the foot like a Taekwondo roundhouse.
- No pivot, so the leg cannot rise and the hips stay square.
- Looking at the floor during the kick.
- Recovering slowly with the leg left out, which invites a catch.
- Leaning so far back that the next technique is impossible.

## Drills

- Bag: kick, return to stance, kick — ten each side, full power, full return.
- Pads: jab–rear body kick; cross–lead hook–rear body kick.
- Pads: the pad holder teeps, you check it and answer with a body kick.
- Balance: hold the kick at full extension against the bag for two seconds before returning.

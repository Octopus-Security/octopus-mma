The cross is the rear-hand straight punch. Its power comes from the ground: the rear foot turns, the hips rotate and the shoulder follows, so the arm is only the last link. In Muay Thai the cross also loads the hips for the next technique: after a cross the weight is turned over the lead leg, which is exactly where it needs to be for a lead hook, or for the rear leg to swing into a low kick once the hips turn back.

## Steps

1. From the stance, lift the rear heel and begin turning it outward.
2. Drive off the rear foot, rotating the hips and then the shoulders toward the target.
3. Extend the rear hand straight to the target, turning the fist palm-down at the end.
4. Keep the lead glove high on the brow, and the chin tucked behind the rear shoulder.
5. Bring the hand straight back to the guard as the hips turn back.

## Key Points

- Rear foot, hip, shoulder, hand — in that order.
- The rear heel turns out; if it stays flat, the hips cannot turn.
- The lead glove stays on your face: the cross opens you to a lead hook or a lead kick.
- Do not overreach. In Muay Thai, leaning onto the lead leg gets it kicked.
- Jab–cross–low kick (1–2–kick) is the first combination every beginner learns.

## Common Mistakes

- Punching with the arm alone, flat-footed.
- Dropping the lead hand as the rear one goes out.
- Lunging forward so the head ends up over the lead knee.
- Leaving the arm out to look at the result.

## Drills

- Shadow: 1–2 at slow speed, freezing at full extension to check the rear heel and lead glove.
- Pads: 1–2–rear low kick, then 1–2–rear body kick.
- Bag: 2–3 (cross–lead hook) for one round, keeping the feet under you.

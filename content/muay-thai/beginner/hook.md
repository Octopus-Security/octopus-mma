The hook is a short curved punch ("mat wiang san", short swinging punch), with the elbow bent around 90 degrees and the arm roughly level, landing on the side of the jaw or the body. The lead hook follows a cross naturally, because the cross leaves the weight loaded over the lead leg. The rear hook is shorter and less common in Muay Thai than in boxing but useful at close range and just before the clinch. The animation shows a lead hook, back to guard, then a rear hook; the arc itself crosses in front of the body, so in side view it reads as the elbow lifting to shoulder height.

## Steps

1. Lead hook: shift your weight slightly onto the lead foot.
2. Pivot on the ball of the lead foot and turn the hips and shoulders to the right; the arm comes along with the body, elbow bent and level with the fist.
3. Land with the knuckles or the palm-down fist, then let the body turn bring the hand back to the guard.
4. Rear hook: turn the rear heel out, rotate the hips to the left, and bring the rear arm around in the same bent-arm shape.
5. Keep the non-punching glove on the brow for both.

## Key Points

- The hook is thrown with the hips and feet; the arm is a fixed shape that rides the turn.
- Elbow at the level of the fist, not below it, for a head hook.
- Keep it short. A wide hook is slow and opens you to a straight punch or a teep.
- The lead hook is the natural follow-up to the cross (1–2–3).
- Lowering the level and hooking to the body sets up the head kick on the same side.

## Common Mistakes

- Swinging the arm wide instead of turning the body.
- Dropping the other hand, which leaves the chin open to a counter.
- Not pivoting the lead foot, which strains the knee and takes the power away.
- Loading up by pulling the arm back first, which telegraphs it.

## Drills

- Shadow: 1–2–3 (jab, cross, lead hook) for a full round.
- Pads: lead hook–rear low kick, using the hook to turn the opponent into the kick.
- Bag: alternate lead and rear hooks at close range with the forehead against an imaginary opponent.
